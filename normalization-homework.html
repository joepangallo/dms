Quick Reference

Understanding 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF

Normalization organizes attributes step-by-step to eliminate repeating groups, partial dependencies, and transitive dependencies.

Normal Form Rules & Conversion Steps
Normal Form Requirement / Goal What is Removed? How to Achieve
1NF (First Normal Form) All attributes must hold atomic (single) values. Every row must be uniquely identified by a Primary Key. Repeating groups & multi-valued fields Expand multi-valued cells into individual rows; choose a composite primary key.
2NF (Second Normal Form) Must be in 1NF, AND every non-key attribute must depend on the entire composite Primary Key. Partial dependencies (attributes depending on only part of the composite key) Move partially dependent attributes into a separate table with the key part they depend on as PK.
3NF (Third Normal Form) Must be in 2NF, AND no non-key attribute can depend on another non-key attribute. Transitive dependencies (non-key → non-key dependencies) Move transitively dependent attributes into a new table with the determinant as PK.

Evidence

Business rules

Treat these statements as facts about every valid row, not just coincidences in the six sample tickets.

TicketEach ticket has one opened date, one customer, one technician, one device, and one labor-hours value.
CustomerA customer ID identifies exactly one customer name and email. A customer may have many tickets.
TechnicianA technician ID identifies exactly one technician name and extension. A technician may handle many tickets.
DeviceA serial number identifies one device type. The same device may return on a later ticket.
Part and supplierA part ID identifies one part name, one current catalog price, and one supplier ID. A supplier ID identifies one supplier name.
Ticket–part combinationA ticket may use many parts. A part appears at most once on one ticket, and its quantity describes that ticket–part pair.
Important price assumptionPART_PRICE is the current catalog price and is determined by PART_ID. It is not a historical price quoted separately on each repair ticket.

4

Convert the design to second normal form

Remove attributes that depend on only part of the composite key.

20 points
  1. Write the relations produced by your 2NF decomposition.
  2. Label every primary key and foreign key.
  3. Under each relation, state which dependency caused you to place those attributes together.
  4. Do not remove transitive dependencies yet; that is the next step.
Reminder: 2NF asks whether an attribute needs the whole composite key. It does not yet remove a non-key attribute that depends on another non-key attribute.
Hint & Worked Example Pattern

Break the 1NF table into separate tables to eliminate partial dependencies. Leave transitive dependencies for 3NF.

Worked Example Pattern
REPAIR_TICKET(TICKET_ID, OPENED_DATE, CUSTOMER_ID, CUSTOMER_NAME, CUSTOMER_EMAIL, TECH_ID, TECH_NAME, TECH_EXT, DEVICE_SERIAL, DEVICE_TYPE, LABOR_HOURS)
PK: TICKET_ID | FK: none
Reason: TICKET_ID determines ticket and customer/tech details (part of composite key).

PART(PART_ID, PART_NAME, SUPPLIER_ID, SUPPLIER_NAME, PART_PRICE)
PK: PART_ID | FK: none
Reason: PART_ID determines part and supplier details (part of composite key).

TICKET_PART(TICKET_ID, PART_ID, QUANTITY)
PK: (TICKET_ID, PART_ID) | FK: TICKET_ID → REPAIR_TICKET(TICKET_ID), PART_ID → PART(PART_ID)
Reason: QUANTITY depends on the full composite key (TICKET_ID, PART_ID).
5

Convert the design to third normal form

Move every remaining fact to the table whose key determines it.

20 points
  1. Write the final set of 3NF relations.
  2. Label every primary key and foreign key.
  3. For each table, complete the sentence: “One row represents …”
  4. Check that every non-key attribute depends on the key, the whole key, and nothing but the key.
Hint & Worked Example Pattern

Remove transitive dependencies (non-key → non-key) by creating lookup tables for Customer, Tech, Device, and Supplier.

Worked Example Pattern
CUSTOMER(CUSTOMER_ID, CUSTOMER_NAME, CUSTOMER_EMAIL)
PK: CUSTOMER_ID | FK: none

PK: TECH_ID | FK: none
