Evidence

Business rules

Treat these statements as facts about every valid row, not just coincidences in the six sample tickets.

TicketEach ticket has one opened date, one customer, one technician, one device, and one labor-hours value.
CustomerA customer ID identifies exactly one customer name and email. A customer may have many tickets.
TechnicianA technician ID identifies exactly one technician name and extension. A technician may handle many tickets.
DeviceA serial number identifies one device type. The same device may return on a later ticket.
Part and supplierA part ID identifies one part name, one current catalog price, and one supplier ID. A supplier ID identifies one supplier name.
Ticket–part combinationA ticket may use many parts. A part appears at most once on one ticket, and its quantity describes that ticket–part pair.
Important price assumptionPART_PRICE is the current catalog price and is determined by PART_ID. It is not a historical price quoted separately on each repair ticket.
Your work

Normalize the data

Complete the steps in order. Relation and dependency notation may be typed as plain text.

1

Diagnose the unnormalized table

Explain what is wrong before changing it.

10 points
  1. State what one source row represents.
  2. Name the repeating group and explain why it violates 1NF.
  3. Give one specific update anomaly, one insertion anomaly, and one deletion anomaly using this repair data.
2

Convert the data to first normal form

Remove the repeating group so every cell holds one value.

20 points
  1. Write the complete 1NF relation, including the six attributes that were hidden inside PARTS_USED.
  2. State what one 1NF row represents.
  3. Identify the primary key. Explain why neither half of that key is sufficient by itself.
  4. State how many rows the six source tickets produce after the repeating group is expanded.
Suggested format: REPAIR_1NF(attribute, attribute, ...) · PK = (...) · one row = ...
3

List the functional dependencies

Use the business rules to identify what determines what.

15 points
  1. List every important functional dependency in the 1NF relation.
  2. Label each one as a full-key dependency, partial dependency, or transitive dependency.
  3. Be sure to include the dependency that determines QUANTITY.
Use arrow notation: determinant → dependent attribute(s) [type]
4

Convert the design to second normal form

Remove attributes that depend on only part of the composite key.

20 points
  1. Write the relations produced by your 2NF decomposition.
  2. Label every primary key and foreign key.
  3. Under each relation, state which dependency caused you to place those attributes together.
  4. Do not remove transitive dependencies yet; that is the next step.
Reminder: 2NF asks whether an attribute needs the whole composite key. It does not yet remove a non-key attribute that depends on another non-key attribute.
5

Convert the design to third normal form

Move every remaining fact to the table whose key determines it.

20 points
  1. Write the final set of 3NF relations.
  2. Label every primary key and foreign key.
  3. For each table, complete the sentence: “One row represents …”
  4. Check that every non-key attribute depends on the key, the whole key, and nothing but the key.
6

Map the relationships

Describe how the final tables connect.

10 points
  1. List each relationship between two final tables.
  2. Give its cardinality as 1:1, 1:M, or M:N.
  3. If an M:N relationship is resolved by an associative table, name that table.
Suggested format: PARENT 1 —— M CHILD (joined on key)
7

Verify the final design

Use the anomalies from task 1 as your test cases.

5 points
  1. Explain where you would update TechSource if its name changed.
  2. Explain how you could add a new part before it appears on a ticket.
  3. Explain why deleting ticket R5002 would not erase the only record of its customer, device, part, or supplier.
